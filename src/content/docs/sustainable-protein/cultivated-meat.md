Cultivated meat is grown from animal cells in bioreactors, without raising and slaughtering animals. The [Good Food Institute](https://gfi.org/cultivated/) and [New Harvest](https://new-harvest.org/) have good primers.

## Where it stands (October 2026)

- **Singapore** was first, approving GOOD Meat chicken in 2020, then Vow's quail in 2024 and Parima's chicken and duck in 2025 ([Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/parima-vital-meat-gourmey-lab-grown-meat-cultivated-chicken-singapore-approval/)).
- **The US** approved Upside and GOOD Meat chicken in 2023, then Wildtype's salmon and Mission Barns' pork fat in 2025 ([Food Processing](https://www.foodprocessing.com/ingredients/alternative-protein/news/55295100/wildtype-is-first-approved-cultured-salmon-launching-in-restaurants)).
- **Australia and New Zealand** approved Vow's quail in 2025 ([FSANZ](https://foodstandards.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/A1269%20Approval%20report.pdf)).
- **The UK** has approved it only for pet food (Meatly, 2024). The Food Standards Agency's sandbox is working through the first applications for people ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2024/07/17/Meatly-gets-UK-approval-for-use-of-cultivated-meat-in-pet-food/)).
- **Bans:** Italy and several US states, including Florida and Texas, have banned its sale ([Perkins Coie](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/cultivated-meat-snapshot-recent-federal-and-state-actions/)).

It's a hard business: Believer Meats closed in December 2025 despite having US approval ([Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/believer-meats-cultivated-lab-grown-meat-shuts-down-lawsuit/)).

## Open questions

- **Cost and scale:** the growth medium is expensive, and nobody has yet produced at the scale of meat.
- **Footprint:** it depends on the medium and the energy. With pharmaceutical-grade medium one study puts it well above beef ([Feedstuffs, on UC Davis research](https://www.feedstuffs.com/news/carbon-footprint-lab-grown-meat-potentially-worse-beef)); with food-grade medium and renewable energy it is projected to be lower.

Cultivated meat may play an important role in replacing meat for people who want meat. But the complexity and cost, without a clear nutritional advantage over single-cell protein, make us think it is best left to well-funded enterprises.
