Plants are a far more sustainable source of protein than meat. Only a fraction of the protein livestock eat ends up in the meat we eat. Per 100 g of protein, beef from beef herds uses about 164 m² of land, peas about 3.4 m² and tofu about 2.2 m² ([Our World in Data, from Poore & Nemecek 2018](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/land-use-protein-poore?tab=table)). In measured UK diets, vegans' food has about a quarter of the greenhouse gas emissions and land use of high meat-eaters' ([Scarborough et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-023-00795-w)).

But crops still need farmland, and with it fertilisers, pesticides and herbicides that run off into rivers. They depend on weather and season, so they are exposed to climate shocks, and there are already populated places where crops can't be grown at all.

Farming can improve a lot. [The Land Institute](https://landinstitute.org/our-work/perennial-crops/) is developing perennial grain crops, and many farmers and growers are raising yields while cutting their environmental impact.

## Plant-based meat

Tofu and tempeh are ancient meat alternatives, bean burgers and veggie burgers took them into fast food, and companies like [Beyond Meat](https://www.beyondmeat.com) and [Impossible Foods](https://impossiblefoods.com) set out to match meat itself. The market has since cooled: Beyond Meat's sales fell through 2025 and it restructured its debt ([Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/beyond-meat-q1-2025-earnings-debt-funding-ahimsa-foundation/)).

We leave plant-based meat to others. It generally offers no nutritional benefit over the plants it is made from, and it carries the same dependence on farmland.
