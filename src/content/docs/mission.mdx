I started AMYBO in 2023 after reading a newspaper article. George Monbiot was arguing that growing protein with microbes, rather than with animals, might be the most important green technology we have, and I went looking for the open source version of it. There wasn't one. There was a lovely open source tempeh fermenter, a blog post by someone else who had gone looking and found nothing, and a great deal of venture-funded work behind patents. So I started one, and people joined.

AMYBO is a not-for-profit, open source community. Our aim is that anyone will be able to produce tasty, nutritious protein at a lower financial cost, and a much lower environmental cost, than by raising animals to eat. The route we've chosen is hydrogen-oxidising bacteria: microbes that make protein from hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide, gases you can make on a bench from water, electricity and a SodaStream cylinder. The [background pages](/background/) explain why we picked them over the alternatives.

## How we work

Everything goes on [GitHub](https://github.com/amy-bo) and the [forum](https://forum.amybo.org/): the designs, the protocols, the results and the mistakes, so that anyone can build on them. We think open source is also the best protection against a good idea being bought and shelved. And we try to keep the kit cheap, because if it costs more than a small lab or a keen amateur can afford, only well-funded labs will use it. That is why the [electroPioreactor](/projects/electropioreactor/), [PioFlo](/projects/pioflo/) and [OpenValve](/projects/other-hardware/openvalve/) exist.

AMYBO is community-led. Biotechnologists, chefs, coders, creatives, engineers, makers, microbiologists, nutritionists and plenty of people who are none of those things all have a part to play. It is currently a not-for-profit trading name of andeye Ltd, so that it can sign contracts and be paid grants; the name and the logo are placeholders for the community to fill in, as the [brand page](/collaborate/brand/) explains.

## Where we are

Imperial College London and the University of Edinburgh are using our kit under the [CARMA Hub project](/projects/carma-hub/), and Edinburgh has the first AEP 0.2 kits. Students built the first units themselves. The medium, the plugin and the designs are all public. What we don't yet have is a strain we can call our own, and that is the next search: sampling rivers and fens and seeing what grows. If you'd like to help with that, [get involved](/collaborate/volunteer/).
