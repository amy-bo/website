I started AMYBO in 2023 after reading a newspaper article. George Monbiot was arguing that growing protein with microbes, rather than with animals, might be the most important green technology we have, and I went looking for the open source version of it. There wasn't one. There were a couple of lovely hobby fermenters, a few blog posts by people who had also gone looking, and a great deal of venture-funded work behind patents. So a few of us decided to start.

AMYBO is a not-for-profit, open source community. Our aim is that anyone will be able to produce tasty, nutritious protein at a lower financial cost, and a much lower environmental cost, than by raising animals to eat. The route we've chosen is hydrogen-oxidising bacteria: microbes that make protein from hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide, gases you can make on a bench from water, electricity and a SodaStream cylinder. The [background pages](/background/) explain why we picked them over the alternatives.

## How we work

- **In the open.** Every design, protocol, result and mistake goes on [GitHub](https://github.com/amy-bo) and the [forum](https://forum.amybo.org/), under licences that let anyone build on them. Open source is also, we think, the best protection against good ideas being bought and shelved.
- **Affordable tools first.** If the equipment costs more than a university lab can find down the back of the sofa, nobody outside a well-funded lab will use it. Hence the [electroPioreactor](/projects/electropioreactor/), [PioFlo](/projects/pioflo/) and [OpenValve](/projects/other-hardware/openvalve/).
- **Community, not company.** Biotechnologists, chefs, coders, creatives, engineers, makers, microbiologists, nutritionists and plenty of people who are none of those things all have a part to play. AMYBO is currently a not-for-profit trading name of andeye Ltd, so that it can sign contracts and be paid grants; the name and the logo are placeholders for the community to fill in, as the [brand page](/collaborate/brand/) explains.

## Where we are

Two universities, Imperial College London and the University of Edinburgh, are growing hydrogen-oxidising bacteria on our kit under the [CARMA Hub project](/projects/carma-hub/). Students have built the kit themselves. The medium, the plugin and the designs are all public. What we don't yet have is a strain we can call our own, and that is the next search: sampling rivers, fens and lochs and seeing what grows. If you'd like to help with that, [get involved](/collaborate/volunteer/).
