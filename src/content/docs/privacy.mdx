This notice explains how AMYBO uses your personal data when you register for an event on this website, or apply for remote access to one of our electroPioreactors.

## Who we are

The data controller is **andeye Ltd, trading as AMYBO**, a private limited company registered in Scotland (company number SC665704), registered office 60 Ladeside, Newmilns, East Ayrshire, KA16 9BE, United Kingdom. Contact us through [our contact form](https://contact.andeye.com/?source=amybo.org&subject=Privacy%20question) or by post at that address.

## What we collect

When you register for an event we collect:

- your name and email address
- whether you will attend in person or remotely, any lab tour you book, and any optional session such as dinner (including a waiting-list place)
- optionally, your organisation or affiliation
- optionally, any dietary or access needs you tell us about
- optionally, your answer to the event's extra question (for example, plans for the day after)
- the time you gave consent and confirmed your email address
- whether you agreed to share your email address with session hosts
- a record of the emails and calendar invitations we sent you

Dietary or access needs can reveal information about your health or beliefs. Only share what you are comfortable with, and only what we need to make the event work for you.

Link pages (amy.bo/~name, a page of someone's links) count how often the page is opened and each link is clicked. We store only the page, the link and the time: nothing about you, no IP address and no cookie.

If you have a link page, we also keep your email address (to send you sign-in links), your name, what you put on the page, and when you last signed in. Your page and everything on it is public; your email address is not. Ask us and we delete the page and all of it.

## Live lecture questions

Lectures can ask the audience live questions at [amybo.org/glasgow](/glasgow). Answers are anonymous: we store the answer, the time, and a random number your browser makes up so that you answer each question once and can change your answer. If you answer a lecture's team poll, the team you chose is kept on your phone and stored with each answer you send after that, and is shown with the answer. We do not store your name, email or IP address; to stop abuse we keep a scrambled (keyed hash) form of the address for up to an hour. Answers are shown on the lecture slides and deleted 30 days after the lecture. Free-text answers with offensive words are never shown, and the presenter can hide any other. You can also raise your hand or send the presenter questions and comments from the same page: these go to the presenter only, never on the slides, and are kept as sent, with that random number and the time, so the presenter has a record (for example, to report abuse to the course team). They are deleted 30 days after the lecture too.

## Remote access to electroPioreactors

When you apply for remote access at [amybo.org/remote](/remote/) we collect your name and email address, your institution and course, which experiment you asked for, why you would like access, and when you gave consent and confirmed your email address. We then record our decision (which experiment we assigned you to, or that we turned the application down) and when we emailed you the link. We use these details **only to decide on your application, give you access to the experiment you are assigned to, and email you about it**. Our lawful basis is your **consent**, which you give by ticking the box on the form. We do not use your details for marketing.

The organisers receive an email when you confirm your application (your name, institution and course, and why you would like access). If we assign you to an experiment, your email address is added to the list of people allowed to sign in to that bioreactor, in **Cloudflare Access**, which guards it: when you sign in, Cloudflare emails you a one-time code and records the sign-in (your email address, the time, your IP address and approximate location) in our Cloudflare account, where only the organisers can see it. Cloudflare also sets a strictly necessary cookie on the bioreactor's address that keeps you signed in for the session. The bioreactor's own software records in its experiment log what was changed and when, but not who changed it. Its camera points at the bioreactor; live video is not recorded, and the bioreactor may save still pictures of the culture with the experiment's data.

**How long we keep it.** An application whose email address is not confirmed is deleted after 3 days. Every other application, including turned-down ones, is **deleted 30 days after the course it is for ends** (or 180 days after you applied, if no end date has been set), and from the day after the course ends nobody but the organisers can sign in to its bioreactor. The database backup described below keeps a deleted application for up to 30 days more. Cloudflare keeps its sign-in records under its own retention policy.

**Withdrawing.** Every email we send about your access has a link to withdraw your application, which deletes it straight away and stops you signing in. You can also [contact us](https://contact.andeye.com/?source=amybo.org&subject=Withdrawing%20a%20remote%20access%20application).

## Why we use it and our lawful basis

We use your data **only to organise the event you registered for and to email you about it**: confirming your registration, sending joining instructions and updates, managing places and waiting lists, and planning catering and access.

Our lawful basis is your **consent**, which you give by ticking the box on the registration form. For any dietary or access needs you tell us about, which can be health or belief information, it is your **explicit consent**, given by the same box. We do not use your details for marketing, and we do not add you to any mailing list.

The talks are recorded so that people who could not come can watch them. Our lawful basis for recording is our **legitimate interest** in sharing the talks; the recordings show the speakers, and questions from the room or from remote attendees may be audible. Recordings are kept and may be published on AMYBO's YouTube channel. You can object at any time: ask your question in the Meet chat, or tell us on the day, and if you are identifiable in a published recording and would rather not be, contact us and we will edit or remove it.

**Asking us not to share images of you.** You can tick "please don't share images of me at this event" when you register or on your registration page. We will then email you, and use our best endeavours to keep you out of the photos and recordings we publish. You may reply with a photo of yourself so that we can check our official photos and recordings before publishing them; we use it only for that, and delete it once we have checked them (and in any case within 30 days after the event). If you join online, please keep your camera off. We cannot control what other attendees photograph or share, so please tell them directly too if it matters to you.

## Who can see it

Only the AMYBO organisers can see the full registrations. The host of each session you attend (for example a lab tour leader) is emailed the list for that session whenever it changes, so they can plan it: your name and whether you have a place or are on the waiting list. Your email address and organisation are included only if you tick the optional box to share them with session hosts; you can change that at any time with your "Manage my registration" link. Hosts are asked to use the list only for that session and to delete it afterwards. We use these service providers to run the website and send email:

- **Cloudflare** hosts the website and the registration database, and provides spam protection (Turnstile, which checks your browser and IP address when you submit the form; we do not store your IP address). The database is held in Cloudflare's **EU jurisdiction**, so registrations are stored and processed in the European Union.
- **Resend** sends our emails, from its EU region.
- **Google Workspace** hosts the organisers' mailbox, which receives a notification when someone registers, changes or cancels (with their name, organisation and registration details), and which organisers may use to send a message to registrants using blind copies.
- **Imperial College London**, which hosts the event, receives the access needs of people attending in person only where that is needed to arrange access to the building, and never dietary information unless catering is arranged with them.
- **Google Meet** streams the talks. If you join remotely, Google processes your name and email address under its own terms when you join the call.
- Registrations are handled by **Events&I**, open source registration software that runs on our own Cloudflare account; no other organisation receives your data through it.

For remote access, **Cloudflare** also runs Cloudflare Access, which checks who may sign in to a bioreactor (above), and the encrypted tunnel that connects the bioreactor to the internet without exposing it directly.

Cloudflare, Resend and Google act as data processors on our behalf. Where they process data outside the UK (the EU for the registration database and email sending; Google and Cloudflare's support and security operations may involve the United States), transfers rely on the UK adequacy regulations for the EU and on the UK International Data Transfer Addendum to each provider's standard contractual clauses elsewhere. We do not sell or share your data with anyone else. If we name speakers on an event page, it is only with their agreement; attendees are never listed publicly.

## How long we keep it

An unconfirmed registration stops holding a place if the email address is not confirmed in time; it is kept, so it can still be confirmed, until it is deleted with the rest. All registrations for an event, the record of emails sent to them, the lists sent to session hosts and the hosts' own contact details are **deleted from our registration system 30 days after the event ends**. The database keeps a point-in-time backup for up to 30 days more (Cloudflare D1 Time Travel), so a deleted record can be restored from backup for that long and is then gone. Notification emails about registrations in our organisers' mailbox are deleted within 30 days after the event. Resend keeps its own delivery logs under its retention policy.

## Withdrawing consent and your rights

You can **withdraw your consent at any time** by cancelling your registration with the "Manage my registration" link in any of our emails. This deletes your details from the registration system straight away (they remain only in the 30-day backup described above). You can also [contact us](https://contact.andeye.com/?source=amybo.org&subject=Withdrawing%20consent%20for%20an%20event%20registration).

You have the right to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict or object to its use, and to data portability. To exercise any of these rights, contact us. If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at [ico.org.uk](https://ico.org.uk/).

## Cookies

This website does not use tracking or advertising cookies. If you edit a link page, signing in sets one strictly necessary cookie that keeps you signed in for up to 60 days, until you sign out. Cloudflare Turnstile, which protects the registration form from spam, may set a strictly necessary cookie to do that job. Videos from YouTube and Vimeo load only when you click play (YouTube) or with Vimeo's do-not-track setting, so their cookies are not set just by visiting a page. Video thumbnails are loaded from YouTube's image server, which receives your IP address.
