The mixed-culture electroPioreactor is where all of this started: a standard Pioreactor vial with an electrode pair through the cap and a CO₂ line, and none of the sealing that makes the [AEP](/projects/electropioreactor/aep/) aseptic. It is cheaper and simpler, and it suits the job we first built it for, which is enrichment. Take a spoonful of mud or river water, put it in a chloride-free medium with hydrogen and CO₂ as the only food, and see what grows. Whatever keeps growing through a few rounds of that, and stops when the electrolysis stops, is very likely a hydrogen-oxidising bacterium, and it might be one nobody has grown before.

The MEP was the only electroPioreactor until the AEP came along. We picked it up again in 2026 as the low-cost version, and built two (MEP 0.3, known to us as ed04 and ed05) for the [School of Biological Sciences](https://biology.ed.ac.uk/) at the University of Edinburgh, for its 2026 MSc industrial projects.
